# Who Was Mary Slessor?

**Source:** Ligonier (Simonetta Carr)
**Date:** 2026-07-14
**URL:** https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/missionary-mary-slessor
**Read time:** ~4 minutes

## TL;DR

Mary Slessor (1848–1915) was a Scottish Presbyterian missionary to Nigeria who became legendary for her willingness to abandon Western precautions — walked barefoot, drank unboiled water, shunned mosquito nets — in order to reach the Okoyong people deep in the interior. She became the **first woman** to hold the position of British vice-consul, presiding over a native court (1892). Her family became the **twins she adopted** to save them from tribal infanticide. By the time she died at 62, she was as well-known as her role model, David Livingstone.

## The making of a missionary

**Born:** December 2, 1848, Gilcomston (suburb of Aberdeen), Scotland. By age ten, her father's alcoholism and unemployment forced her to work as a weaver in Dundee's textile mills — 12-hour shifts. After work she improved her slim education and served as a missionary teacher at her local United Presbyterian Church.

**Called.** In 1873, captivated by reports following David Livingstone's death, she applied to her denomination's foreign mission board. With only minimal training, she was off to Duke Town, Calabar, near the southeastern coast of present-day Nigeria, in 1876.

**Language and gender work.** Spent time with local women to learn Efik. Was surprised none had yet joined the church. _"Something more must be done for the women here if we are to raise the men."_ Teaching the women and improving their oppressed condition remained a lifelong commitment.

**Up-country.** Asked permission to move three miles inland (1885). When her mother and sister died that year, she considered her homeland ties irrevocably severed: _"Heaven is now nearer to me than Britain, and no one will be anxious about me if I go up-country."_ Eventually settled in Okoyong.

**Marriage and the mission board.** In 1891, Charles Watt Morrison — 18 years her junior — proposed. They asked the board to allow Morrison to join her in Okoyong (not the reverse). She left the matter to the Lord: _"If it be for His glory and the advantage of His cause there to let another join in it I will be grateful. If not, I will still try to be grateful, as He knows best."_ The board refused. Engagement off.

> "Slessor's greater emphasis was on the gospel she conveyed to everyone she met, resting on God's power to transform hearts."

## The twins: family formed through rescue

Some African tribes believed twins were the product of evil-spirit couplings and killed them (and their mothers). Slessor worked to prevent these murders until the local chief declared the custom unlawful. Her family **became** the twins she adopted.

## Influence and legacy

- **1892:** British government, impressed with her interaction with locals, appointed her **vice-consul of the region** (presiding over the native court). First woman to hold such a position.
- **1905:** Vice president of the Ikot Obong native court.
- Settled quarrels unconventionally — once told two warring chiefs, _"I will prick your hand and his, and you can suck each other's blood"_ (per Mina Amess, who lived with Slessor).
- Years of hard work + exposure to the elements → painful rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Died:** January 13, 1915, age 62, of fever and dysentery.

Her image appeared on a 1997 Clydesdale Bank £10 note. In Nigeria she is remembered as _Obongawan Okoyong_ — **Queen of Okoyong**.
