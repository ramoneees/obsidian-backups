# 26 People, One Morning: How to Build a Same-Day Event Dossier With AI

**Thanh Pham** — An Arena Hall member texted at 8am needing 26-person dossiers for an event that morning. By event time, he had a Google Doc with a summary on every person.

## The same-morning build
Combining two things: **ChatGPT agents** for the reasoning layer and **Lindy** for the data-gathering layer.

**Step 1: ChatGPT agents for summarization.** Feed the list of names with clear instructions: who is this person, what are they known for, current company and role, recent items worth knowing. Batch — not one at a time.

**Step 2: Lindy for structured research.** Custom Lindy that takes each name and runs it through:
- Perplexity (recent news and summaries)
- Google Search (anything published or featured)
- LinkedIn (current role, background, connections)
- Lindy aggregates the data from each source.

**Step 3: Output to Google Doc.** Single doc, one section per person, clean format, ready to read.

For 26 people, this ran in the time it would have taken to manually research two or three.

## Why it matters
> "Most people have done some version of the manual process. You head into a dinner or a panel and you spend 20 minutes googling the other people in the room. You scan their LinkedIn. You try to remember something worth bringing up. Half the time you run out of time and walk in blind."

> "What AI lets you do is compress the research time enough that you can actually do it consistently, not just when you happen to have an hour free the night before."

## The proactive version: runs before you ask
The same-morning build is reactive — someone remembered to ask. The better version runs without prompting.

Every morning at 7am, a briefing lands in your inbox: meetings today, brief on every attendee, LinkedIn, anything recent in the news. Thirty minutes before each call, a second nudge with just the key context for that meeting. Walk into every room already knowing who's in it. Without having to think to ask.

> "The Austin membership club I work with runs something similar. The founder used to spend chunks of his morning digging through emails, documents, and calendar notes just to prepare for what was coming. Now he gets a structured briefing that pulls everything together — meeting context, attendee backgrounds, logistics — before he's finished his first cup of coffee."

## Building the proactive version
- Calendar access so the system knows who you're meeting
- The same research workflow, triggered by each upcoming calendar event
- A delivery mechanism (email or Slack works fine)

Takes a few extra hours to set up properly. Once it's running, it runs before you think to ask.

> "The Arena Hall member texted me at 8am needing 26 dossiers. He got them. But the better version is the one where he never needs to text at all — because the system already knows he has an event today."
