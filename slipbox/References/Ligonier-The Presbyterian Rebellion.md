# The Presbyterian Rebellion

**George Grant** — originally published for America's 250th.

> "There is not a single instance in history in which civil liberty was lost, and religious liberty preserved entire." — John Witherspoon

## The British verdict
250 years ago, news arrived in London that a long conflict with the American colonies had culminated in a declaration of independence. Horace Walpole in Parliament: "There is no use crying about it. Cousin America has eloped with a Presbyterian parson." King George III was convinced the patriot leaders were Presbyterians (loosely including Reformed Congregationalists). William Jones to the king, 1776: "This has been a Presbyterian war from the beginning." Ambrose Serle to the Secretary of State: the Revolution was a "religious war." A Hessian captain: "Call this war… by whatsoever name you may, only call it not an American Revolution, it is nothing more nor less than an Irish-Scotch Presbyterian Rebellion."

## The theological engine
The Great Awakening (1720–1750) put Reformed theology and scriptural warrant for liberty in the same pulpits. Favorite texts: 2 Cor 3:17 ("Where the spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty") and Gal 5:1 ("For freedom Christ has set us free"). Isaac Backus: the gospel of grace could only take root in a culture that allowed for the freedom to proclaim it. George Whitefield in Portsmouth, 1774: "I can't in conscience leave the town without acquainting you with a secret. My heart bleeds for America."

## John Witherspoon
"The Presbyterian parson" was John Witherspoon — president of the College of New Jersey at Princeton, a Scottish scholar-preacher. He developed Princeton's curriculum (French, history, international law, philosophy, speech) within a consistent Reformational worldview, planted dozens of churches along the frontier, and lent his reputation to the cause of independence. He was "as high a Son of Liberty, as any Man in America" (John Adams).

Of his 478 Princeton graduates:
- 12 members of the Continental Congress
- 5 delegates to the Constitutional Convention
- 1 vice president
- 49 House members, 28 senators, 3 Supreme Court justices
- 1 Secretary of State, 3 attorneys general, 2 foreign ministers
- **James Madison** — "Father of the Constitution," 4th president

## The Dominion of Providence sermon
Two months after his election to the Continental Congress, Witherspoon preached "The Dominion of Providence Over the Passions of Men" at Princeton — subsequently published in Philadelphia. It made a strong biblical argument for the Declaration of Independence based on the covenantal violations of king and Parliament as evidence of God's providential dealings. It rested on a Reformational theology of resistance — Viret, Knox, Rutherford, Milton — built on **magistral interpositionalism** and **covenantal jurisdictionalism**.

> "Resistance to tyrants is obedience to God."
