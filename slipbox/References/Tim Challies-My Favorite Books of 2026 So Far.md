# My Favorite Books of 2026 So Far

Tim Challies curates a mid-year reading list — three picks from the Christian market and three from the general market — for summer reading.

## Christian market (3)

1. **_[The Desecration of Man](https://amzn.to/3SXGyrM)_ — Carl Trueman.** Continues the argument from _The Rise and Triumph of the Modern Self_ and _To Change All Worlds_: why is popular entertainment so hostile to Christian ethics? Why has the West normalized the celebration of abortion and the mocking of faith? The Olympics' Last Supper parody and drag-queen story hours are symptoms Trueman traces to the rejection of God.

2. **_[A Heart Aflame for God](https://amzn.to/4oYK2q7)_ — Matthew C. Bingham.** A distinctly Reformed-evangelical approach to spiritual formation. "Reformation Protestants already have a deep, compelling, and satisfying tradition to draw upon" — at a time when many Protestants are taking cues from other traditions.

3. **_[30 Key Moments in the History of Christianity](https://amzn.to/3QztTuB)_ — Mark W. Graham.** A global tour of "Our People" in the first millennium — from the Apostles through 1000 AD. Graham's stories span the Black Sea, Red Sea, Mediterranean, the Middle East, Britain, and China. Fills the gap Reformed readers tend to skip (Apostles → Councils → Reformation).

## General market (3)

4. **_[Shooting Up: A Memoir of Love, Loss, and Addiction](https://amzn.to/3QMehDq)_ — Jonathan Tepper.** Not a Christian book, but "as Christian a book as I've read this year." Tepper's parents were missionaries to Madrid who pivoted from reaching intellectuals (L'Abri-style) to reaching heroin addicts. A poignant, compelling, sometimes heartbreaking story of family and ministry.

5. **_[The Zorg: A Tale of Greed and Murder That Inspired the Abolition of Slavery](https://amzn.to/4wmkNQL)_ — Siddharth Kara.** A notorious slave ship that jettisoned enslaved people to save drinking water. The insurance claim that followed sparked the legal and public-opinion battle that led to abolition in England. "You will be proud to read of the Christians who led the way in this noble fight for justice."

6. **_[The Wide Wide Sea](https://amzn.to/4oYOrJF)_ — Hampton Sides.** Captain Cook's third voyage — the search for the Northwest Passage. Riveting narrative of exploration and exploitation. Sides is "at times antagonistic toward Christianity" but the storytelling carries the reader through.

## Additional recommendations
- Rick Atkinson's [Liberation Trilogy](https://amzn.to/4b5C9cx) (WWII European theater)
- _[Neptune's Fortune](https://amzn.to/4gQ6gbA)_ (shipwrecks and recovery)
- _[Sister, Sinner](https://amzn.to/4v9kk3A)_ (Aimee Semple McPherson biography)
- _[Rasputin](https://amzn.to/4yiZMbR)_
- _[Everybody Loses](https://amzn.to/4vIe4AX)_ (rise of sports gambling)
