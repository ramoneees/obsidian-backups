# How Can I Deal with Fear?

**Virgil Walker**

## The shape of adult fear
Fear doesn't disappear at adulthood. It just gets dressed up:
- The man who fears poverty doesn't walk around frightened — he is *careful*, *responsible*, calling it *stewardship*
- The woman who fears being thought poorly of doesn't feel afraid — she is simply *attentive to what people need*, *agreeable*

In both cases, fear has become a posture they live from. It is closer to who they are than to anything they consciously think. The people around them can see it before they can. **His wife could name it. He cannot.**

## The disciples in the storm
The disciples could not name it either. The storm swallowed the light, the water was in the boat, and the Man who had told them to cross the sea was asleep on a cushion in the stern. They woke Him with an accusation dressed as a question: "Do you not care that we are perishing?" (Mark 4:38). Their fear felt like sober judgment. So it was a strange thing for Jesus to still the wind and turn the question back on them: "Why are you so afraid? Have you still no faith?" (Mark 4:40).

> "Jesus was not addressing the storm. He was addressing what the storm had exposed. Their fear revealed where their confidence rested: They trusted the wind to destroy them more than they trusted the One who made the wind. That is what fear does before it is anything else. It reveals what we love and what we trust."

## The cure is not less fear but right fear
Trying to feel braver never works for long. You cannot calm a fear without dealing with the love underneath it, and you cannot reorder your loves by willpower. The question is not how to feel less afraid, but *what* you are afraid of and *whether it is the right thing to fear*.

> "Scripture's answer to fear might come as a surprise. It does not tell the fearful to stop fearing. It tells them to fear rightly: 'The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge' (Prov. 1:7). When God prepares His people for a season of dread, He does not pretend their fears are imaginary. He says they are misdirected: 'Do not fear what they fear, nor be in dread. But the Lord of hosts, him you shall honor as holy. Let him be your fear, and let him be your dread' (Isa. 8:12–13). A man who truly fears God has little fear leftover for anything else, because the One he reverences most cannot be threatened by the things that frightened him before."

## The gospel flip
"Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell." Then, without pausing: "Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? And not one of them will fall to the ground apart from your Father… Fear not, therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows" (Matt 10:28–31).

The storm that terrifies you is not on the loose. It is in His hand.

> "Faith is not the absence of fear. It is fear that has finally found its rightful object."
