# When Life Gets Busy, Keep Abiding

**Source:** Desiring God (Cara Ray)
**Date:** 2026-07-14
**URL:** https://www.desiringgod.org/articles/when-life-gets-busy-keep-abiding

## TL;DR

The Martha-and-Mary story (Luke 10:38–42) reorders our productivity instincts: **holy leisure comes before holy service**. Augustine's phrase _"amor veritatis requirit otium sanctum; necessitas caritatis susceptum negotium iustum"_ — "the love of truth seeks holy leisure; the necessity of love undertakes righteous activity" (City of God 19.19). We don't withdraw from work; we **reorder it** so Christ is sought first where our lives naturally unfold.

## The frame: which comes first?

Martha welcomed Jesus into her home. Mary sat at his feet. Martha complained when her priorities weren't followed. Jesus's response — _"Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her"_ — reorders our _this-then-that_ reflexes. **Sitting at Jesus's feet takes precedence over serving him.** Before she fed others, Martha needed her own soul fed.

## Holy leisure: definition, not absence

Modern leisure = escape or entertainment. **Augustinian leisure** = active pursuit of goodness, truth, beauty. The soul filled with "love of truth" is not idle — it is drawn into **restful devotion to Jesus**. As we engage spiritual disciplines, our souls find rest. Leisure, then, is **not the opposite of meaningful work but the condition of the soul that makes our work meaningful**.

> "Our irritation often reveals an attempt at righteous activity without first seeking holy leisure."

## Holy leisure in everyday life

Not monastic withdrawal. Three practical spheres:

### 1. At home — focus on your first attention

Martha was right to serve. But because she hadn't first oriented her heart to Jesus, she was anxious. We blame our frustrations on schedules and interruptions. The deeper diagnosis: **righteous activity without first seeking holy leisure**.

Practice: begin the day with God and his word. Return to him at key points. Ask to sit at his feet even while on the move. Pause to pray throughout the day. Ask God to be your **holy leisure — the wellspring from which holy service flows**.

### 2. At work — focus on whom you serve

Martha focused on completing tasks instead of being in Jesus's presence. At work, cultivating holy leisure often requires **tapping the brakes before hitting the gas**. When we put holy service before holy leisure, we miss Jesus — the one thing necessary.

Define success not by productivity but by **faithfulness to the one we ultimately serve**.

> "Holy leisure is not an excuse to do nothing, but a reordering of our priorities that puts first things first." (Matt 6:33)

### 3. In community — focus on presence over performance

Martha likely felt cultural expectations of hospitality. Mary saw the unique privilege of receiving Jesus's undivided attention. We slip into a performance mindset: serving to be seen and approved rather than simply loving Christ and loving others. **Choosing presence over performance** looks like slowing down, listening attentively, genuinely enjoying the people God has placed around us.

## Righteous activity for his name's sake

Mary's focused attention was her first priority. Jesus called it the good portion. By sitting at his feet before she served, she enjoyed a spiritual meal she would remember long after the physical one was forgotten. **Mary chose Jesus over her sister, her guests, and the cultural expectations of her as a hostess** — and Jesus affirmed this as the right choice.

We may not always live up to Mary's example. But we too can cultivate holy leisure in the middle of everyday life. As we sit at his feet and **seek first his kingdom, our priorities are reordered**. Our _this-then-that_ approach doesn't have to be at the mercy of our circumstances.
