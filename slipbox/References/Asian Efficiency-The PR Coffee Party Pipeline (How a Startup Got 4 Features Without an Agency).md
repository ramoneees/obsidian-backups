# The PR Coffee Party Pipeline (How a Startup Got 4 Features Without an Agency)

**Thanh Pham** — Lucas Siegel (Yuna, AI mental health, $30M valuation, 50K users, partnerships in progress with Amazon and T-Mobile) built an automated PR pipeline over a weekend and got featured in **four Austin news outlets**. No agency, no retainer.

## Why most PR pitches fail
> "The typical PR approach is spray-and-pray. You write a press release, compile a media list from a database or past contacts, and blast it out to as many journalists as possible. The pitch is generic. The press release is written in the third person and reads like a legal document."

> "Journalists receive dozens of these a day. Most get deleted before they're even opened."

> "The ones that get read — and the ones that turn into features — share something in common: they feel personal. They show that the sender actually read the journalist's work. They connect the story to something the journalist has already been covering."

Lucas calls it the "coffee party" model: "you treat [a journalist] like someone you'd want to grab coffee with. You'd never walk up to someone at coffee and pitch them without knowing anything about them. But that's what most PR pitches do."

## The four-step pipeline

**Step 1 — Journalist Research**
ChatGPT deep research to identify journalists actually relevant to your story. For Yuna, that's mental health + workplace wellness + Austin business + HR tech + AI in healthcare — a specific intersection.

**Step 2 — Data Enrichment**
A Lindy agent scrapes email addresses, professional contact info, Twitter/X handles, and their articles from the last 3–6 months. What they covered, what angles they explored, who they quoted.

**Step 3 — Personalized Outreach**
Each email opens with a direct reference to something the journalist actually wrote — not a compliment, not "I've been following your work." A specific reference. Then it connects the company story to that angle.

> "Editing is faster than authoring. The journalist doesn't need a generic press release — they need something they can quickly understand and connect to their beat."

**Step 4 — Conditional Follow-ups**
- No open after 2 days → different subject line
- Opened, no click or reply → short "just wanted to make sure this didn't get buried" follow-up
- No response after follow-up → let it go

**Result:** Four features in Austin news outlets.

## The economics
Traditional PR agency hourly rate: $200–400/hr. For a list of 50 journalists, that's many hours. The automated version runs on AI API calls that cost cents. The quality of personalization is arguably higher, because the research step is instant and the agent can pull more context per journalist than a human would.

> "The part that remains genuinely human — and should — is the story itself. The angle. What makes this company interesting enough to write about. What connects its work to what journalists are already covering."

> "That's the part the pipeline can't do for you. But once you have a story worth telling, the pipeline does the distribution work."

## Building your own
1. **A clear story angle** — what's actually interesting
2. **A journalist research step** — ChatGPT deep research
3. **A data enrichment agent** — Lindy, Clay, or similar
4. **An email template with personalization fields** — the core template stays consistent, the opening line adapts
5. **Conditional follow-up sequences** — one follow-up is appropriate, two is pushing it, three is probably spam

> "The key insight: editing is faster than authoring. When a human reviews and approves AI-drafted personalized emails instead of writing from scratch, they can process 10x more outreach in the same time without sacrificing quality."
