# He Tried Paid Ads, Social Media, and Google. A QR Code at the Hair Station Did More.

**Thanh Pham** — case study of a Fort Worth salon owner named Chris (20+ years, multi-location, "the unicorn" of his industry).

## The setup
Chris tried the usual growth playbook — paid social, Google ads, promotions. None of it moved the needle. Then he put a **QR code at every stylist station**.

When a client finishes their appointment and loves their hair, the stylist hands them a **$15-20 product sample** as a thank-you for leaving a Google review right there while they're still in the chair.

**Result:** over six months, the salon went from ~400 new clients/month to 800+. Not from a clever ad. Not from a viral post. From a QR code and a small gift, executed consistently by the right person at the right moment.

## Why it actually worked
Two reasons:
1. **The person asking.** The stylist is the relationship — knows hair history, color formula, kids' names. The front desk knows the credit card number. "When the front desk asks a client to prebook, they often hear: 'I don't know my schedule. My kids are out of school…'" When the stylist asks, the same client says: "Whatever you say."
2. **The moment.** "Right after a great haircut. Right after a good session. Right after a problem gets solved. That's your window."

## "Every guest, every time."
Chris's operating philosophy in four words. Not most guests. Not when you remember. **Every guest, every time.**

Concrete implementation:
- Scoreboard metrics hourly in the back break room — service sales, product sales, prebooking percentages, all handwritten
- Team contests and monthly leaderboards
- Chris himself nudges stylists on the messaging app when reviews slow down
- "The base rule never changes: every guest, every time."

## Translation for any service business
1. **Identify your peak moment** (when client is most satisfied — right after the haircut, right after a problem gets solved).
2. **Find the right person to ask.** The person the client actually trusts. Make it their job — with the right support and reminder.
3. **Make it worth their while to say yes.** Tangible exchange, not a favor.
4. **Run it every time, not most of the time.** Design so it's easier to do than to skip.

> "The difference between a good idea and a business system is that the system runs even when nobody's thinking about it."
