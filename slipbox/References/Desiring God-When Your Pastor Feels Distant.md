# When Your Pastor Feels Distant

**John Piper** answers an anonymous pastor of a small-but-growing church. "When your pastor feels distant" is, Piper says, "one of the good problems of a growing church."

> "The first thing to say is that I don't think I ever got this exactly right. I don't even know what 'exactly right' looks like when it comes to every member being known and cared for and held accountable by biblically qualified church leaders in a church — say, like ours, which was growing from three hundred to four thousand over thirty years."

## Four biblical guidelines

**1. Lead Like a Shepherd** (1 Pet 5:2–3)
> "Shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight, not under compulsion, but willingly, as God would have you; not for shameful gain, but eagerly; not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock."

**2. Love with an Open Heart** (1 Thess 2:7–8)
> "We were gentle among you, like a nursing mother taking care of her own children. So, being affectionately desirous of you, we were ready to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves, because you had become very dear to us."

> "There are personality types that probably should not be pastors."

**3. Seek Out the Informal** (Acts 20:20)
> "I did not shrink from declaring to you anything that was profitable, and teaching you in public and from house to house."

**4. Joyfully Keep Watch** (Heb 13:17)
> "Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you."

## Embodying biblical truth
> "I always viewed the pulpit as my primary way of shepherding the people… All of that I saw as what you do in the pulpit. Some of those don't go elsewhere only. I think the pulpit is where emotional health and humility and strength and transparency begin in the local church. If they don't see it there, where are they going to see it?"

Sunday morning at Bethlehem was "the Mount of Transfiguration, where the disciples fell down on their faces before the glory of the Lord (Matt 17:1, Mark 9:2)." Sunday evening was "the Mount of Olives, where Jesus stretched out on the grass on his elbow with his disciples, and spoke to them about how things were going."

## Known and accountable
As the church grew from zero to 40 elders, he divided into small groups with trained small group leaders and accountable elders. He lingered after services to pray with people — "the last person to walk out of the sanctuary, absolutely exhausted, because standing there and praying with people is more taxing than preaching."

> "You can number the people in hundreds who did that, instead of thousands — but all of them knew about it. And that made a huge difference in the way they perceived me and my availability, my shepherd bent."
