# How to Build a Content Idea Bank With a Google Sheet (2026)

**Source:** Asian Efficiency (Thanh Pham)
**Date:** 2026-07-25
**URL:** https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/content-idea-bank-google-sheet/

## TL;DR

Most small-business content creation fails not from lack of motivation or time, but from **blank-page friction**: starting from nothing every Tuesday and skipping instead. The fix is a **content idea bank** — a Lindy agent runs every Sunday, takes your brand pillars, generates 8 ideas (caption + photo prompt), appends to a Google Sheet you keep open. When you're ready to post, you **pick from options** instead of authoring from scratch. Real case: Michelle (personal chef) went from posting whenever she remembered to posting several times a week from a self-refilling backlog. Setup takes an hour, then the bank refills itself.

## The diagnosis: it's not motivation, it's friction

Most owners know they should be posting. Want to. Thought about it. Then Tuesday arrives, they open the phone, and stare at a blank page. Workflow becomes:

> Scroll for a photo → open ChatGPT → ask for a caption → post something generic → repeat (or, more often, don't).

The piece argues **this isn't a motivation problem or a time problem — it's a friction problem.** Starting from nothing every time is hard enough to skip.

## The case: Michelle the personal chef

Beautiful product, interesting story, no social presence. Same blank-page workflow as everyone else. Three brand pillars emerged from conversation:

1. Health and nutrition.
2. Memories that food creates.
3. Buying back time for clients by handling meals.

Built her an idea bank based on those three pillars. She went from posting whenever she remembered → several times a week from an automatically-refilled backlog.

## How the system works

**Every Sunday morning**, a Lindy agent runs automatically:

1. Takes her three brand pillars.
2. Generates **8 content ideas** (each with caption + suggested photo prompt).
3. Appends to a Google Sheet kept open on her phone.

When she's ready to post: open the sheet → pick what fits the week (photos she took, dish she just made, client story) → finish the caption if she wants → post.

She still makes every decision. Still adds her voice. The only thing that changed: **she starts with options instead of a blank page.**

## Why "options vs. blank page" is the whole thing

Starting from blank page means doing **several things at once**:

- Decide what to post about.
- Think of an interesting angle.
- Write a caption.
- Check brand fit.
- Hope you're not overthinking it.

That's a lot of cognitive load compressed into a moment when most people want to do the quick thing and move on.

Starting from **8 options**, you do **one thing: pick.** The decision is already scoped. Creative direction is already there. **You're evaluating, not generating.** That's a fundamentally different kind of work.

## The broader pattern

Most AI content advice is about prompting — how to write a better prompt, how to get better output from ChatGPT. That's useful but doesn't solve the **structural problem**: you still have to go find the tool, start the prompt, and do it from nothing each time.

> "A system that runs in the background and keeps your idea bank stocked solves a different problem. It changes the relationship between you and your posting workflow from 'start from scratch' to 'pick from options.'"

## How to build one (4 steps)

1. **Define your content pillars** — 2–3 themes genuinely central to your business. Not aspirational; *real* ones. What do your clients come to you for?
2. **Build a Lindy agent** (or any automation tool) that runs on a schedule — Sunday morning works well — and generates a set of ideas based on your pillars. Prompt it to include suggested angle + caption draft + photo prompt.
3. **Append to a Google Sheet** (or any document you actually open regularly).
4. **Each week, review the sheet, pick what fits, add your voice, post.**

Setup: 1–2 hours first time. After that: bank refills itself weekly; "what do I post?" question becomes near-zero cost.

> "That's the blank page problem, solved."
