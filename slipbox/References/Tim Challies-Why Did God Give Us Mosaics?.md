# Why Did God Give Us Mosaics?

A short, beautiful devotional on beauty, brokenness, and the God who makes mosaics.

## The image

In the great European palaces, entire walls are covered with mosaics. Pieces of broken glass, pottery, fragments of seashells — arranged by the finest artisans into images, portraits, and landscapes. From a distance, you think you're looking at paintings. Only up close do you see the surprising medium.

> "If I were to hand you a box filled with shattered glass, another filled with shards of pottery, and a third filled with broken seashells, and then tell you to create something beautiful, you might scoff at me and doubt that it could ever be possible."

## The application

There is so much in life that is broken, incomplete, or seems like waste. Bodies that broke down too soon, wealth stolen or squandered, opportunities never realized, dreams unfulfilled, sins committed by and against us, loves taken, trials added. We wonder whether good can ever come from such shattering.

But: **maybe God gave us mosaics to assure us that it is indeed possible.**

If it is possible for mere humans — if even weak and faltering hands can bring order from chaos and beauty from brokenness — how much more for the almighty God? And if magnificent art can be made from rocks, shells, and glass, how much more from the most precious medium of all — human lives?

> "Take heart, Christian, that the God who *gave* us mosaics is in the business of *making* mosaics. He sees beauty beyond our marred natures, he sees possibility beyond our fractured circumstances, he sees ability beyond our broken bodies, and so he shapes it, he assembles it, and he polishes it into something wondrous, something worthy of his name."

(Inspired in part by an illustration Challies found in the works of De Witt Talmage.)
