# Scotland and the Birth of the United States

**S. Donald Fortson III** — Tabletalk, originally published 2014.

## The Scottish lineage
Scottish Presbyterianism — robust theology, government by elders, strict piety — would shape America through the **Scots-Irish** (Ulster Scots) immigrants who became the backbone of the Revolutionary era. By 1776, nearly **500,000 Scots-Irish** had come to America. They populated the Virginia and Carolina Piedmont via the "Great Philadelphia Wagon Road"; by 1750 they had reached the South Carolina Piedmont and north Georgia. Scottish Highlanders settled along the North Carolina seaboard and coastal Georgia.

## Francis Makemie (1657–1708)
The "Father of American Presbyterianism." Ordained 1682 by the Irish Presbytery of Laggan, departed for Maryland 1683. Established five congregations in Maryland/Virginia/North Carolina. Founded the first American presbytery in 1706 in Philadelphia. His 1707 acquittal in New York (after Lord Cornbury arrested him for illegal preaching) was a major religious-liberty milestone.

## The Great Awakening + Princeton
The First Great Awakening (1720s–40s) was "the most remarkable spiritual event" shaping Scots-Irish colonists. Whitefield and Edwards deepened American passion for freedom to worship according to conscience. In 1747, pro-revival Presbyterians chartered the **College of New Jersey** to train ministers. After Jonathan Edwards served briefly as president, **John Witherspoon** (1723–1794) — a Scottish minister — was selected and arrived in 1768.

## Witherspoon's Revolutionary role
- First political sermon (May 1776): resistance to tyranny as obedience to God
- Hessian officer: "Call this war… only call it not an American rebellion; it is nothing more or less than a Scotch Irish Presbyterian rebellion"
- Signed the **Declaration of Independence** (July 4, 1776), the Articles of Confederation, helped ratify the Constitution (1787)
- Served on Board of War and Board of Foreign Affairs
- Lost two sons in battle
- Most prominent student: **James Madison** ("Father of the Constitution")

> "Fighting for religious liberty is about as quintessentially Scottish and American as it gets."

## Witherspoon's influence (selection)
- 1 vice president
- 12 members of the Continental Congress
- 5 delegates to the Constitutional Convention
- 49 representatives, 28 senators, 3 Supreme Court justices
- 1 president (Madison)
