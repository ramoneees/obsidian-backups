# Human Tragedies, Divine Purposes

**R.C. Sproul** (1939–2017) — on the Christian doctrine of providence and the "why" question behind every tragedy.

## The question
"Why did this happen?" is the question that burns in everyone. The Christian worldview cannot accept meaningless events: at its heart is the idea that everything in history has a purpose in the mind of almighty God. God is a purposive God; He is not chaotic. As Einstein once remarked, God does not "play dice" with the universe.

> "If someone were to say to me: 'Why did this happen? What was God's purpose in all of this?' the only honest answer I could give would be, simply, 'I don't know.' I can't read God's mind. If you were to ask me, 'Was God involved?' my answer, of course, would be yes… But what His specific purpose was in this event, I do not know."

## The blind man (John 9)
The disciples met a man born blind. They asked: "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" (John 9:2). Jesus recognized a **fallacy of the false dilemma** — reducing possibilities to two when there was a third. He answered: "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be displayed in him" (John 9:3).

> "God's purpose here was to demonstrate who Jesus was. And to this day, two thousand years later, that blind man… sits with them and talks about how God used his blindness to demonstrate the identity of Christ. He discovered that his tragic condition was by no means senseless. It had a divine purpose that has borne witness to Christ through all history."

## The honest posture
Sproul admits to a specific epistemic limit: he does not know the *why* of any individual tragedy. What he knows: God is involved, his purposes are real, and the answer to the "why" often comes only in retrospect — in this life or the next.
