# Human Tragedies, Divine Purposes

**Author**: R.C. Sproul
**Published**: July 6, 2026
**Original URL**: https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/human-tragedies-divine-purpose

## The Core Question

When tragedy strikes — especially as a Christian theist — we ask: "How could God allow this?" or "Why, God, did this happen?"

Christians cannot allow for meaningless events. At the heart of the Christian worldview is the idea that **everything in history has a purpose in the mind of almighty God**.

> "God is a purposive God; He is not chaotic." — Albert Einstein on God not "playing dice"

Honest answer to "what was God's specific purpose?": **I don't know.** God's involvement: yes, by doctrine of providence. The specific why: beyond our access.

## The Text: John 9

Disciples see a man blind from birth. Ask: "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?"

Jesus identifies a **logical fallacy** — the *fallacy of the false dilemma* (either/or). The disciples reduced possibilities to two when there was a third they hadn't considered.

Jesus: "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be displayed in him."

## The Application

The lesson is not "suffering has a specific reason we can decode." It's that the false-dilemma framing — "either this is God's punishment or it's meaningless" — is the wrong question.

A third option exists: **God has purposes we cannot fully see, but purposes nonetheless**.

We are still pressed to ask the "why" question. The Christian answer is honest: yes, God was involved; yes, there was purpose; no, we may not be able to identify the specific purpose this side of eternity.
