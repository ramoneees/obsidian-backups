# Gen-Z Revival Isn't Happening. Here's How It Could.

**Source:** The Gospel Coalition (Zac Fuller)
**Date:** 2026-07-14
**URL:** https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/gen-z-revival-how/

> **Author note:** Zac Fuller works in marketing and communications at Samford University. He and his wife live in Birmingham, Alabama, and are members of Redeemer Community Church. Article indexed on TGC via his [author profile](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/profile/zac-fuller/).

## TL;DR

The UniteUS movement on college campuses has produced striking visuals — students baptized in truck beds at Samford's football stadium until after midnight. Stories of Gen Z returning to Christian faith surface weekly. **But the data tells a different story: Gen Z remains decidedly secular.** At best, we are seeing a **leveling off of the decline**, not a revival. The missing piece: **embodied, personal faith practice**. Online evangelism may spark, but without churches seeing Gen-Z congregants, "revival" remains a rally cry, not a reality.

## When did it end?

Some of Gen Z's most formative years began in **spring 2020, with COVID-19**. Isolation + technological dependence + disillusionment. Truth — about vaccines, police brutality, public health policies — constantly shifting. Cultural gatekeepers unprepared to answer honestly.

That dissatisfaction lingers. As Paul Ainleitner [points out](https://paulanleitner.substack.com/p/the-war-that-never-ended), there was **no concrete endpoint** to the pandemic. Pandemic hangover: distrust for institutions — but not always the cynical stripe. Many insist something steadier and more unmoving is out there, _if only we knew how to find it_.

## Voices crying out

Fuller's own search started with political podcasts. Daily listener to conservative-leaning shows. YouTube/Instagram algorithms then served cultural content — movies, celebrity drama. **Then Christian content.**

> "Day after day, I returned to this well for solutions… While scrolling, I stumbled on content produced by Christians. At first, I'd watch, distantly interested in their presentations of the gospel. But the Spirit used these channels to soften my heart and set it on fire for Christ."

**The criticism of "Christian influencers" is not hard to understand.** Their platforms have produced scandals, cover-ups, false prophets. The algorithm makes no distinction between transcendental and conspiratorial; it drops them both into our timelines side by side. **Discernment is required; not every self-appointed voice deserves an audience.**

But set the worst cases aside. **For a generation chronically online — being marched toward the despair that waits at the end of every road of disillusionment — those faithful voices are a lifeline.** Their work matters precisely because that is where this generation already is.

> "Among all the voices competing for the attention of young people, one of them will win. If we want Gen Z to see Jesus as the truth, can we as the church get behind the ones crying out, 'Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand'?"

## The missing piece

Fuller's faith didn't take root until he walked into a church as a young adult. Weekly meetings with a college pastor → irregular Sunday attendance at different churches → finding a church home → serving → **learning what it meant to take up his cross**.

> **This is perhaps the missing piece when we talk about revival.**

A General Social Survey study found **weekly church attendance among 18–34-year-olds in decline**. Another study, recording since 1973, shows the percentage with "hardly any" confidence in organized religion at one of its highest points. **There may be good evangelism online, but churches overall aren't seeing more Gen-Z congregants.**

**Until we help the people of Gen Z see the value of embodied, personal faith practice, revival will remain an online rally cry rather than a reality.**

Hebrews 10:25 sets a clear standard: regular meetings with the body of Christ. **Neglecting to meet with one another is how we remain siloed and subject to whatever our algorithms decide to feed us next.** Without one another, who are we meant to encourage to good works, to care for, to love? Maybe a better question: **who are we meant to receive all of these things from?**

> "Isolation is the way of the world. Community is the way of the cross."

## Two ways

How did Fuller become a Christian? **Ernest Hemingway's bankruptcy line:** _"Two ways. Gradually and then suddenly."_
