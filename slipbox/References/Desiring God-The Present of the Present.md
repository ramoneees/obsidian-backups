# The Present of the Present

**Clinton Manley**

> Your life is like a series of rooms connected by the hall of Time. Behind you stretches a long line of past places… Before you, the hallway branches in a hundred directions, presenting a parade of rooms you might move through… In this series of rooms, the only one you can live in is *now*.

The piece opens with a poem that captures the "absurd impulse" of trying to live anywhere but the present.

## The door to elsewhen (the past)
- The past is familiar — we know it, lived in those rooms, packed with trophies
- "Many of us spend our whole lives banging our heads against those doors, trying to lay hands on those *heaps of golden oncewhens*"
- Like the Lotus Eaters or Miss Havisham, we get intoxicated by the past
- Solomon: "Say not, 'Why were the former days better than these?' For it is not from wisdom that you ask this" (Eccl 7:10)
- C.S. Lewis in *Letters to Malcolm*: "God shows us a new facet of the glory, and we refuse to look at it because we're still looking for the old one."

> "Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the appetite" (Eccl 6:9).

## A fiction yet to be (the future)
- "For a temporal creature like man (it surely is very different for God), the future is a mirage: lovely to look at, impossible to touch; always coming, never arriving."
- Pascal (*Pensees* 36): "We almost never think of the present, and if we do think of it, it is only to see what light it throws on our plans for the future… *Thus we never actually live, but [only] hope to live*."
- Screwtape: "He wants men to be concerned with what they do; our business is to keep them thinking about what will happen to them."
- "Sin thrives on the fiction of the future. Sin's promises (which it never keeps in the present) depend on a fanciful time to come."

## The present of the present
> "If you want to be holy now, you must be wholly now. All of our God-given duties impinge on the present. The past is no help. No amount of past faithfulness will keep you from sin (think of David). No amount of past wisdom will keep you from being a fool (think of Solomon). No past experiences of spiritual mountaintops will keep you in the faith (think of Judas). And the future is still a fiction that will have its own highs and lows (Matt 6:34)."

- "Only Now is sacramental": God gives grace only for the current cross. "Give us this day our daily bread" (Matt 6:11). Like manna, yesterday's stock is gone, and you cannot hoard any for tomorrow.
- Solomon (Eccl 5:18–20): "to eat and drink and find enjoyment *in* all the toil… this is the gift of God."

> "If you want to be happy and holy and truly live, learn the discipline of being present. Let the past go; let the future worry about itself. Put down your phone; lift up your eyes. There's no better (or other) time than now to attend to God and this *Gift of grace and glory*. This is our lot."

## Notas e Conexões
- A useful complement to [[Tim Challies-God Believes In Rest. Do You?]] — Manley makes the "live in the now" case theologically, Challies makes the "rest is a gift" case
- The Screwtape / Pascal quotes are strong pastoral ammunition against chronic anxiety
- "Sin thrives on the fiction of the future" — one of the better one-liners on present-moment sin management
