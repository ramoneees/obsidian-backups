# Notion vs. ClickUp (2026): Which Is Actually Cheaper?

## Quick Verdict

- **Notion wins** on docs, knowledge management, and flexible databases — and is **cheaper with AI included** ($18/user vs. ClickUp's $21/user).
- **ClickUp wins** on native time tracking, goal/OKR tracking, and real Gantt charts with dependencies.
- **Neither is a great pure task manager** — for solo task tracking, use Todoist instead.

## Pricing Snapshot (July 2026)

| | Notion | ClickUp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free plan | 1 user only | Unlimited users |
| Entry paid | $10/user/mo (Plus, annual) | $7/user/mo (Unlimited, annual) |
| Mid-tier | $18/user/mo (Business, annual) | $12/user/mo (Business, annual) |
| AI included? | Yes, on Business | No — $9/user/mo add-on |
| **Business + AI total** | **$18/user/mo** | **$21/user/mo** |

## Where Notion Wins

- **Docs and knowledge management.** Notion's DNA is documents; pages nest cleanly, the interface works like a connected wiki, not a task manager that bolted on a text editor. AE uses Notion as the central operating system for team knowledge: SOPs, client-facing resources, onboarding docs.
- **Databases that flex.** One database viewed five ways (table, board, gallery, calendar, timeline, list). ClickUp has multiple views too, but they feel like separate features.
- **AI included in Business.** In May 2025 Notion killed the separate AI add-on and bundled unlimited AI into Business — *Notion Business + AI = $18/user/mo, ClickUp Business + Brain = $21/user/mo*. Notion is actually cheaper at the AI tier despite its "premium design-first" reputation.
- **Cleaner design, less overwhelm.** ClickUp has a reputation for feature creep (settings inside settings). Notion's default is a blank page.

## Where ClickUp Wins

- **Native time tracking.** Built-in timer logs directly to the task, with time reports, estimates, and estimated-vs-actual. Notion has no time tracking at all — you need Toggl/Harvest/whatever and end up managing two systems. *Matters a lot for client services teams that bill by the hour.*
- **Goal and OKR tracking.** Native Goals feature links tasks/projects to higher-level goals, with rollup progress. Notion can approximate with databases + formulas, but it's not native.
- **Gantt charts and project structure.** ClickUp's Gantt view is a real Gantt — dependencies, drag-to-reschedule, critical path. Notion's Timeline view is basic.
- **More views, more structure.** Workload view (who's overloaded?), Map view, Whiteboard, Everything view.

## AI Billing Gotcha

ClickUp's Brain is **charged per paid seat in the workspace, not per user who actually uses AI**. 10 members, 3 heavy users → still $9 × 10 = $90/month. Notion: $18/user flat.

## The Migration Pain Problem

Both tools have the same trap: once you're inside, leaving is hard. SOPs, databases, team wikis in Notion = serious migration project. Project history in ClickUp = reconstructing workflows from scratch. Use both free plans for a real project, not a tutorial, before committing.

## Who Should Pick Which

**Get Notion if:** docs/guides/wikis are your primary work; you're a content team, agency, or knowledge-heavy operation; you want AI bundled without a separate line item; flexibility > opinionated structure; design/UX matters.

**Get ClickUp if:** your work is project-driven with deadlines and client deliverables; you need time tracking without adding another tool; OKR tracking is something you actually use; you need Gantt + dependency management; you manage a team's workload across multiple simultaneous projects.

**What Thanh uses:** Notion for AE's knowledge layer and client-facing resources. Personal task management = Todoist (not either of these). ClickUp was evaluated seriously and found over-engineered for his needs.

## The Honest Note About Tasks

Neither is the best pure task manager. Both will frustrate you before they help — they're platforms that require investment before they pay off. For individual task management, Todoist still wins. The comparison only makes sense when the real question is "I need a company wiki and a place to manage projects for my 5-person team."

The decision isn't about feature count — it's about whether your team thinks in **documents first** (Notion) or **projects first** (ClickUp). And the AI pricing math is non-obvious: Notion with AI is *cheaper* than ClickUp with AI, which most comparison articles miss.
