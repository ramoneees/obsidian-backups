# What Is Idolatry?

**Source**: Ligonier (learn.ligonier.org)
**Author**: Erik Raymond
**Date**: 2026-07-21
**URL**: https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/what-is-idolatry
**Read time**: 3 min

## TL;DR

Idolatry is worshiping anything created instead of the Creator. God is concerned with idolatry because God is concerned with worship. Far from being a narcissistic demand, this is the right ordering: God alone deserves worship, and protecting His glory is part of being God. Paul describes idolatry as an _exchange_ — trading the truth about God for a lie and worshiping the creature rather than the Creator (Rom 1:25).

## Key Points

### What idolatry is
When people hear "idolatry" they picture either ancient rituals with carved statues, fan "idolizing" celebrities, or metaphor for obsession. But at root: **idolatry is worshiping anything created instead of the Creator.**

### Why God is concerned with idolatry
God is concerned with idolatry because God is concerned with worship. Far from being a narcissistic demand:
- If God were a created being, it would be unhealthy to demand worship.
- But as Creator, God made us to worship Him. This is right.
- God alone deserves worship; failing to promote and protect His glory is failing to act like God.
- "The promotion of creation and the demotion of the Creator are both inappropriate. It's idolatry."

### Idolatry as exchange (Rom 1:25)
Paul: "they exchanged the truth about God for a lie and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever! Amen."

Exchanging God's glory for idols is like appraising God's value on a scale. Instead of seeing His infinite weight, the worshiper determines that something created (lighter, emptier) outweighs Him. The image: a person concluding a feather weighs more than an elephant. Only possible by "exchanging the truth about God for a lie" (cf. Rom 1:18).

### The Ten Commandments open with idolatry (Ex 20:2–3)
> "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me."

The placement is intentional. The first commandment is the foundation for the other nine; to disobey the others is to turn away from the true God to worship an idol.

### Biblical examples (warning signs)
- **Exodus 32**: the people redeemed from Egypt fashioned a god from their gold and worshiped it.
- **1 Kings 12:28**: King Jeroboam, fearing he'd lose power, replicated the idolatry — invented his own system of worship. "Behold your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt."

> "God hates idolatry because He loves His glory. God loves His glory because it is good."

### How varied idolatry actually is
We may think of it as bowing to a statue, but idolatry can also be "desiring something you don't have that someone else has" (cf. Ex 20:17). The first commandment's reach is much wider than the obvious case.

### Continuing in the fight
- We turn away from idols to serve the living God (1 Thess 1:9).
- But this is not the end of the fight. Continue to turn away from idols (1 Cor 10:14).
- Work to keep ourselves _and others_ from them (1 John 5:21).
