# Count the Cost of Missions, but Don't Forget the Joys

**Source**: Desiring God
**Date**: 2026-07-21
**URL**: https://www.desiringgod.org/articles/count-the-cost-of-missions-but-dont-forget-the-joys
**Original DB URL**: http://rss.desiringgod.org/link/10732/17383910/count-the-cost-of-missions-but-dont-forget-the-joys

## TL;DR

Missionary service is costly — isolation, distance from family, language acquisition, sickness, rejection, persecution. Counting the cost is wise. But counting the cost doesn't mean serving out of self-reliance: missionaries serve in dependence on the particular grace God gives for the calling. The reward ("an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison") makes any cost worth it.

## Key Points

### Count the cost

Missionary service involves real hardship:
- Isolation, distance from family and friends
- Long years of language and culture acquisition
- Sickness, rejection, persecution
- Resilient people broken by tragedy or ground down by slow accumulation of difficulties

It's right, wise, and necessary to count the cost before committing.

### Depend on grace

Counting the cost doesn't mean expecting to serve out of your own strength. Missionaries serve in dependence on the **particular grace** God gives to fulfill their calling.

- Paul: "minister according to the grace given to us" (Romans 12:6).
- "Grace given" = God's generous _appointment_ and _empowering_ of his people for ministry (1 Cor 3:10; Eph 3:8; Eph 4:7).
- This grace includes the Spirit's power (Acts 4:33; 6:8) and the ability to cheerfully endure hardships (Romans 12:8 — "mercy with cheerfulness").
- The Macedonian churches gave cheerfully for the relief of the saints "in a severe test of affliction" (2 Cor 8:1–4). Paul doesn't want people to give or serve unless this cheerfulness is present (2 Cor 9:7).

The grace-enabled question: "Can you still beg earnestly for the favor of taking part when you encounter severe tests of affliction?" (2 Cor 8:2,4)

> "Whatever hardships we suffer for Christ are temporary. Our reward is forever."

### Look to the reward

Paul in one of his hardest seasons: "this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison" (2 Cor 4:17).

The reward lasts forever. The cost is temporary.

### The marriage analogy

Starting missionary service is like starting out in marriage. If someone on the airplane asked "why marry Kim even though marriage includes hardship?" — the answer is "Sure, we might face difficulties, but they're nothing next to the joys of being with her!" When the reward is great enough, we don't fear the cost.

Don't send would-be missionaries a discouraging "the excitement won't last" message. The excitement may come and go, but for those God has gifted, the reward — and even many parts of the journey to that reward — will be far better than those first few steps. And the reward will last forever.
