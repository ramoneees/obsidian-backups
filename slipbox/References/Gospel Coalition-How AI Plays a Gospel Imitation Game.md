## The core distinction: imitation vs. participation

Watkin's framing (per Hansen): the difference between **imitation** and **participation**. AI can imitate intelligence, creativity, conversation, even love. But participation — mutual indwelling, gift-and-response, vulnerability — is what AI cannot share. **Mutuality and reciprocity** are the specifically human (or more carefully: creaturely-in-relationship) features that distinguish personhood from performance.

## Why this matters for the church

- **Christian anthropology isn't threatened by AI intelligence.** What makes humans image-bearers isn't computational power but the capacity for relationship with God and neighbor.
- **A frictionless life can become passive rather than free.** When AI removes all friction, the soul atrophies — both in attention and in virtue.
- **Transhumanism** as the temptation to outgrow humanity: secular tech culture's substitute eschatology.
- Studying AI has made Watkin **more excited about God** — because the limits of AI reveal, by contrast, the gifts of being human: embodiment, contingency, dependency, love received and given.
