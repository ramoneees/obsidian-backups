# The Little White Church Is Empty

**Source:** The Gospel Coalition
**Date:** 2026-07-14
**URL:** https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/little-white-church/

## TL;DR

Across rural America, the small white country church is dying — **attendance collapsing, buildings decaying, pastors retiring without replacements**. Cedar Street Church in **Tipton, Iowa** draws 30 of the town's 3,000 residents. The pattern is widespread: **rural America is one of the next mission fields ripe for harvest**. Yet some young pastors are answering the call — Gabe Lockin (25, Iowa City) drives an hour each Sunday to preach at Oakville Christian Church (200 residents); Jordan Howell gave up a university church plant to serve eight small-town families in Urbana. **A third of America's 350,000 churches may close in coming years.**

## Cedar Street, Tipton, Iowa

Jerry and Wendy Goldsmith's church is dying. About 30 of Tipton's 3,000 residents attend Sunday service. Building constructed late 19th century, slowly falling apart. No money for repairs. Pastor retired last June; interim minister since. Almost all congregants over 60.

**The pattern is widespread.** Many rural churches suffer major attendance decline — partly due to shrinking small-town populations, partly because traditional country churches aren't effectively reaching younger generations and struggle to fill pulpits.

> "Rural America is one of the next mission fields ripe for harvest." — Tim Lubinus, executive director, Baptist Convention of Iowa

## The structural problem

- **Population collapse.** The Iowa town where Lubinus grew up: 500 residents as a teenager, now ~half. His high-school town: 1,600, now shrunk by nearly half. _"When you have a 50 percent reduction in population, it's hard to keep church attendance."_
- **Pastor shortage.** Pastoral salary expectations rising; willingness to take small-congregation pay cut falling. Many churches rely on temporary fixes.

## Oakville: a 25-year-old and his friends

Oakville, Iowa: under 200 residents. When Oakville Christian Church's pastor retired last winter, **Gabe Lockin** (25, Iowa City) got a text from seminary friend Justyn Wyatt. Eight pews. ~20-30 people in winter, 30-40 in summer.

> "It depends on the weather."

Most congregants are farmers. Someone rattles off old hymns like _"Happy the Home When God Is There"_ on the upright piano; an elder lights altar candles with a brass lighter. Mother's Day: the five children pass out flowers to their moms. After-service coffee and homemade cookies.

The church **split from the United Methodist Church in 2023** to hold its biblically sound doctrine on marriage. Severed from money and pastor network. But confident God will provide. Lockin preached his last sermon there in June; Oakville still has no full-time pastor.

## The data

Ryan Burge (political scientist, religious trends): **almost three in five Iowans go to church less than once a year.** _"That's not a particularly religious state."_

One study estimates **a third of America's ~350,000 churches will close in the next several years** — about 100,000. Tens of thousands of closures certain, many rural.

## Hope: Urbana, Iowa

Six years ago (during COVID), eight small-town families started meeting near Urbana, Iowa. Inside a garage, live-streaming services from Veritas Church in Cedar Rapids, longing for their own pastor.

At the same time, **Jordan Howell**, a Veritas pastor, was preparing a university church plant with Salt Network. February 2024: Veritas elders decided to help the Urbana families become autonomous. Needed a pastor. Howell tagged along; someone joked, _"Well, hey, if your church plant doesn't work out, you could always come to Urbana!"_

That night, driving I-380, Howell couldn't shake the idea. _"It's so biblical. You have sheep without a shepherd. It's right in front of my face."_

Salt Network executive director Ryan Hill told him: _"I don't think there are a lot of young men your age who feel called and equipped to do rural church planting."_ Howell decided in **three weeks**.
