# The Real Reasons We Don't Read the Bible

**David Mathis** — first in a series titled "Why Don't You Read the Bible?"

## The crisis
- One study: 84% of American adults read for 5 minutes or less per day
- Lifeway: only 31% of churchgoers read the Bible daily — that's <10% of all American adults
- Romans 10:14–17: "How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard?… So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."
- "Life and death hang on our ability to hear God — and to hear God, we have to read."

## Beyond "I'm too busy"
Mathis looks past the surface excuses to the deeper causes. The series will address each in turn, but here are a few:

1. **General apathy toward divine revelation.** We live in an increasingly illiterate culture. Many couldn't effectively read the Bible if they wanted to (illiteracy), and they don't even want to (apathy). Mark 4:18–19: "the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches and the desires for other things enter in and choke the word."

2. **Stubborn pride and spiritual immaturity.** "Being too proud to submit to anyone or anything besides themselves, they do not receive the word with meekness (James 1:21), and so they forfeit Scripture's life and power." Hebrews 5:12: "Though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic principles of the oracles of God. You need milk, not solid food."

3. **Functional illiteracy.** People lack the basic tools for reading well. Proverbs 2:1–5: "if you call out for insight and raise your voice for understanding, if you seek it like silver and search for it as for hidden treasures, then you will understand the fear of the Lord."

4. **Dwindling attention spans.** Even with basic reading tools, modern people struggle to sit and focus for 60–90 seconds. Psalm 119:35–37: "Turn my eyes from looking at worthless things; and give me life in your ways." Mary > Martha (Luke 10:41–42).

> "Life and death hang on our ability to hear God — and to hear God, we have to read."

## The 2 Kings 22 invitation
When the high priest Hilkiah found the Book of the Law in the temple, the secretary read it to King Josiah — who tore his clothes (2 Kings 22:11). We don't know how long it had been lost, but it was devastating to discover the neglect. Mathis hopes this series is a 2 Kings 22 moment for readers.

> "What stability might more regular Bible reading bring to your troubled soul? What peace to your distress, what counsel for your confusion, what perspective for your disorientation, what hope for your future? Why don't you come and read the Bible with us?"
