# Works & Wonders (July 5)

A weekly column Challies runs: a brief devotional + curated interesting links from the week. Six items this week.

## 1. Our Being and God's

Devotional reflection on the distinction between God's self-existent being and ours as created/derived. Cites Thomas Goodwin: "as a shadow is to the object that casts it — no less real, but only a pale, imperfect imitation." "True Being belongs only to the One who has always been and will always be." Anchors on Acts 17:28 ("In him we live and move and have our being").

## 2. John MacArthur's Influence
A [YouTube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PE7THOBMxgw) of prominent Christians (H.B. Charles, Al Mohler, Paul Washer, others) describing how MacArthur shaped their ministry. A short tribute to a defining figure of confessional, expository, complementarian evangelicalism.

## 3. Where There Are No Rats
[How Alberta Eradicated Rats](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/albertas-war-on-rats) — a Canadian curiosity piece. "Alberta, a Canadian province, is rat free, and has been for more than seventy years." Geographic advantages help, but the province has put enormous sustained effort into the boundary. "After Antarctica, Alberta is the largest rat-free area on Earth."

## 4. Hand of You
[handofyou.app](https://handofyou.app/) — draw on your screen, get a font of your own handwriting back, so you can add a personal touch to typed letters. Neat.

## 5. The Story of SermonAudio
[Short documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wx0Fq1-Dcio) on SermonAudio's history and how it has remained effective and future-proofed against potential censorship. A useful history of a tool many pastors and laypeople lean on.

## 6. Hear Now Our Hearts
New Celtic Worship track: "[Hear Now Our Hearts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKeF820DlGU)."

## Notas e Conexões
- The MacArthur tribute lines up with [[TMAI Together We Pray]] (the same network MacArthur helped found)
- The Goodwin quote on being is a useful anchor for any work on divine simplicity, classical theism, or [[Asian Efficiency]]'s "automation removes ceilings" — different domains, same idea of receiving the gift rather than manufacturing it
