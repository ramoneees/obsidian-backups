# The Strange and Wonderful Love of Christ

**John Piper** message text on John 11:1–6 — Lazarus, Mary, and Martha.

> My text is not Galatians 2:20, but my theme is this: *What is it like to be loved by Jesus*? My text is John 11:1–6. We are going to see the *strange* and *wonderful* love of Jesus. We will see it in the context of death and glory — so look for them.

## The setup
Mary and Martha send word to Jesus that Lazarus is sick — "Lord, he whom you *love* is ill." Jesus's first response: "This illness does not lead to death. It is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it" (John 11:4). "It is not mainly about death, though he will die (and Jesus knows he will die). It is mainly about *God*."

## The disquieting love
John underlines the love three times in verses 2, 3, and 5: "Now Jesus *loved* Martha and her sister and Lazarus." Verse 6: "So, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer in the place where he was." *Therefore*, because of this love, he let him die.

> "Jesus is choosing to let Lazarus die and to make sure that he is dead when he gets there — *four days dead*, as it turns out."

Piper insists this is not softened by the resurrection: "Lazarus really died. It is not an easy thing to die. And as far as Lazarus knew, Jesus didn't come. And his sisters saw him die. And they buried him. And they wondered where in the world Jesus was. This was real death and real loss."

## "I am the resurrection and the life"
> "Martha said to him, 'I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day.' Jesus said to her, 'I am the resurrection and the life. *Whoever* believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live, and *everyone* who lives and believes in me shall never die.'"

The way to think about Lazarus's death: it was just as terrible as yours. And your death will not be any more terrible than his, because you too will be raised by Jesus, and the time between death and resurrection is as nothing compared to eternity.

## What is love?
> "Love means giving us what we need most. And what we need most is not healing but a full and endless experience of the glory of God."

> "Love is the longing that labors and suffers to enthrall others with what is infinitely and eternally satisfying: the glory of God."

> "If that is where you stand today — and all of us do, in some sense, not seeing clearly how God is glorified in the death of our beloved — do not judge before the resurrection. God is doing more than you can know. And the resurrection will bring it all to light. In the meantime, trust him, and treasure him above all things. Know that you are loved by him with a strange and wonderful kind of love."

## Notas e Conexões
- One of the most powerful Piper messages on suffering + love. Direct companion to [[Ligonier-Human Tragedies, Divine Purposes]] (Sproul on John 9) — both turn on Jesus's response to suffering and God's hidden purposes
- The Lazarus/your-death parallel is a pastoral tool for grief counseling
- The closing "do not judge before the resurrection" line is one of the most quotable things Piper has written
