# Your Clients Will Say No to the Front Desk and Yes to the Stylist

## The Story

Chris Murphy runs multiple salon locations. Same client, same ask, two very different results:

- **Front desk asks**: "Let me get your next appointment set up."
- **Client says**: "Oh, I don't know my schedule right now. My kids are out of school. I'll call back." *walks out without an appointment*

- **Stylist asks**: "We need to get you in six weeks."
- **Client says**: "Whatever you say."

Chris: "They might say no to the front desk person. But they'll do whatever their stylist tells them."

## Why This Happens

It's not about the wording. It's about **who's asking**.

- The stylist knows her color formula, her past cuts, her schedule, her kids' names
- The stylist has been in the room with her for an hour, entrusted with her appearance
- The front desk knows her credit card and email

When the client says "whatever you say" to the stylist, she's responding to years of a real relationship. The same ask from the front desk has no weight.

## The Pattern Everywhere

The same dynamic exists in nearly every service business:

- Doctor's receptionist reminds you about annual physical → ignored
- Doctor, in the room, says "I want to see you in six months" → booked
- Gym email about renewing membership → ignored
- Trainer, mid-session: "Let's not break the streak" → renewed

**Clients don't respond to organizations. They respond to the person in the room with them.**

## The Business Design Problem

Most service businesses build retention/upsell/communication flows around whoever "should" own those functions:

- Front desk handles scheduling
- Marketing handles promotions
- Manager handles retention

That makes sense on paper. But "supposed to" and "who clients actually listen to" are often different people.

Chris's fix: route **every** client initiative through the stylists — review requests, rebooking, product recommendations, loyalty programs. Not as a mandate, as an acknowledgment of how the business actually works.

## The Question Worth Asking

Every service business has someone like Chris's stylists:

- The person clients trust most
- The one they'd follow to a new location
- The one whose recommendation carries real weight

Sometimes it's the founder. Often it's not. Sometimes it's a specific employee who's been there for years.

**Find that person. Design your systems so the highest-leverage asks route through them.**
